## Step 2: Drafting Workflow

Before writing anything, read the user's real posts above. Every time.

1. **Understand the core message** — what is the user actually trying to say?
2. **Find the concrete hook** — what's the most specific, surprising, or human detail in the story?
3. **Draft in their voice** — lift their actual sentence patterns, not a smoothed-out version
4. **Check for AI tells** (see below) and remove them
5. **Present the draft** with a brief note on the approach taken

---

## Common Post Structures

**Concrete Hook → Real Point**
Open with a specific moment or observation. Land the insight.

**Observation → Practical Tips**
Start with what you noticed. Follow with what it means for the reader.

**Warm Team / Event Update**
Human and specific. Names, moments, what actually happened.

**Contrarian Take + Data**
State the unpopular position. Back it with evidence.

**Tool / Test Breakdown**
What you tried, what you learned, what you'd do differently.

---

## Output Requirements

- 100–250 words
- Paragraph breaks between ideas (not walls of text)
- Sounds like a real person, not a LinkedIn influencer
- Final check: would someone who knows the user read this and think "yep, that's them"?

---

## AI Tells to Avoid

**Phrases:** "I've been thinking a lot about...", "Here's what I've learned:", "The truth is...", "This is your reminder that...", "Unpopular opinion:", "Hot take:"

**Structural tells:** Starting every line as a fragment for drama, 10-item numbered lists, calling out "3 lessons from X", excessive line breaks between every single sentence

**Tone tells:** Humble-bragging disguised as vulnerability, generic inspirational endings, asking "What do you think?" as a default CTA
